Common hydraulic problems

General diagnostic starting points. Follow equipment-specific service and safety procedures.

System runs slow

Check engine/motor speed, reservoir level, filters, pump inlet restrictions, relief valve setting, internal cylinder leakage, and pump flow under load.

Low pressure or weak force

Check the load, gauge accuracy, relief setting, pump condition, valve bypass, and actuator seals. Pressure is created by resistance to flow.

Pump noise or cavitation

Inspect oil level and viscosity, inlet line sizing, clogged strainers, suction restrictions, and air leaks. Stop if severe cavitation is suspected.

Hydraulic oil overheating

Check continuous relief flow, internal leakage, excessive pressure drop, cooling capacity, oil level, and duty cycle.

Cylinder drifts

Investigate piston seal bypass, directional valve leakage, load-holding valves, and external leaks. Secure loads before testing.

Quick formulas

Piston area: π × bore² ÷ 4

Annulus area: π × (bore² − rod²) ÷ 4

Force (lbf): pressure (PSI) × area (in²)

Hydraulic HP: PSI × GPM ÷ 1714

Theoretical pump GPM: in³/rev × RPM ÷ 231

Cylinder speed (in/s): GPM × 231 ÷ (area in² × 60)

Safety reminder

Depressurize and lock out equipment before service. Never use hands to search for hydraulic leaks; high-pressure fluid injection can cause serious injury. Calculations are estimates, not equipment ratings.
